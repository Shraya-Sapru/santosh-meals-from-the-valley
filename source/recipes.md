Kashmiri Pandit cuisine is rooted in Shaivite traditions (followers of Lord Shiva) and Sattvic principles (in conformity with Ayurveda), the kitchen skips onions, garlic and tomatoes, relying instead on a signature quartet of asafoetida (heeng), dried ginger powder, fennel powder and Kashmiri red chilli.

This practice is followed for both vegetarian as well as non-vegetarian dishes, grounded in ancient practices emphasizing purity, slow-simmering techniques and specific spice combinations.

To begin with, our focus will be on two signature dishes each, both from non vegetarian and vegetarian options.

Historically the non-vegetarian dishes primarily used lamb or goat and fish.

- **Rogan josh** – A rich, bright red lamb curry flavoured with fennel, ginger, asafoetida, Yogurt and of course some whole spices as well. 

***Ingredients***

- Mutton : 1 Kg (with bone from shoulder and ribs portion cut into medium pieces

- Mustard Oil: 4 to 5 table spoons

- Asafoetida (Heeng) – pinch/less than ¼ tsp

- Whole spices – 2 bay leaves, 1 inch cinnamon stick, 5 cloves, 1 black cardamom, 4 green cardamom.

- Yoghurt (Curd): 150-200 gms (whisked)

- Ground spices

- 4 tsp kashmiri red chilli powder (for colour and mild heat)

- 2 tsp fennel seed (saunf) powder.

- 1 tsp dry ginger (sonth) powder

- ½ tsp garam masala powder

- Salt : 1½  to 2 tsp/ to taste

- Water: 500 ml – 600 ml – as needed

**Recipe/ Instructions:**

- Heat the mustard oil in a heavy-bottomed pan or wok until it reaches its smoking point. Then let it cool slightly

- Add the bay leaves, cinnamon (break into few pieces), cloves, black/green cardamoms (slightly crushed). Let it crackle a bit

- Add asafoetida, followed immediately by mutton pieces.

- Sear the meat over high heat for 8 to 10 minutes, until it turns slightly brown and the natural juices dry up.

- Add salt, stir in between, lower the heat

- Add Kashmiri chilli powder and mix it well.

- Add the whisked yoghurt mixture to the pan a little at a time stirring continuously to prevent curdling.

- Cook the meat on medium-low heat until the Yoghurt is absorbed and the oil begins to separate.

- Stir in the fennel and ginger powder. Pour in enough water to make sufficient gravy.

- Cover the pan tightly and let it simmer on low heat for about 45 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the mutton is tender and a layer of red oil floats on the top.

- Uncover, sprinkle the garam masala powder and stir gently.

- Let it rest with lid on for 10 minutes and serve hot with steaming boiled/cooked rice.

**Note**: It can be pressure cooked as well. Process is same, except for cooking for 45 minutes, it can be cooked for 2 whistles after adding the water and then for another 5 minutes after reducing heat and wait till the lid gets released on its own.

- **Mutton ****Yakhni** : is a rich yoghurt based mutton gravy and does not make use of turmeric or red chilli powder

***Ingredients ***

- Mutton: 1 Kg (with bone preferably fattier cuts like breast, shoulder or ribs.

- Yoghurt : 500 gms full fat fresh Yoghurt smoothly whisked

- 3 tsp fennel powder

- 1 tsp dry ginger powder.

- Asafoetida – a pinch

- 4-5 green cardamoms and 2 black cardamoms lightly crushed

- 4 to 5 cloves

- 1 cinnamon stick (broken into few pieces)

- 1 tsp cumin or caraway seeds (shahi jeera)

- Mustard oil 4 to 5 table spoons or ghee

- Salt to taste

**Rec****ipe****/Instructions: **

- Boil the mutton in a pot or pressure cooker with water, salt, asafoetida, ginger powder and fennel powder. Cook until the meat is tender

- Keep the mutton on boil and add slowly the whisked curd, stirring continuously to prevent curdling, continue boiling till a smooth slightly thick gravy is formed.

- Heat mustard oil in a heavy bottomed pan until the pungent smoke settles, then add cloves, cinnamon stick, cumin and cardamoms, thereafter, add it immediately to mutton yakhni gravy.

- Boil for few minutes till the surface of the gravy gives a light silky finish.

- Serve hot with steamed rice.

***Vegetarian Dishes***:

- **Kashmiri Dum ****Aaloo**** (Potato Curry)**

This is the most popular vegetarian dish following the same authentic principle of slow cooking in a spice yogurt based gravy.

**Ingredients**

- 12 medium sized rounded potatoes

- Mustard oil – for deep frying.

- Whole spices – 

- 3 Cloves

- ½ tsp cumin (jeera) seeds

- 2 black cardamoms (silted)

- 2 bay leaves

- 1 inch cinnamon stick (broken into pieces)

- A pinch of asafoetida (heeng)

- Powdered spices – 

- 2 tsp kashmiri chilli powder

- 2 tsp fennel powder (saunf)

- ½ tsp ginger  powder

- ½ tsp garam masala

- 1 to 1½ tsp salt (according to taste)

-  3 to 4 tbs Whisked Curd 

**Recipe….. **

- Boil the potatoes in a pressure cooker for 1 or 2 whistle max., let it cool

- Peel it. Pierce each potato several times with a small fork or a tooth pick, ensuring that it doesn’t break and stays as a whole

- Deep fry the potatoes, till they turn a little more than golden brown with slightly wrinkled texture.

In the meantime prepare the gravy –

- Take about 4 tbs of oil in a deep pan or kadai. Heat it. Add cloves, cumin seeds, black cardamoms, bay leaves, cinnamon, asafoetida (heeng), sauté it for 1 or 2 min. on a slow flame.

- Add Kashmiri chilly powder. Mix it well.

- Add the whisked curd and keep on a stirring it continuously till the gravy thickens and starts leaving the oil on the sides.

- At this stage add all the potatoes in the pan and let it cook without stirring it for 2 min. on slow heat with lid on.

- Add water in the pan, sufficient enough to cover all the potatoes

- Add fennel powder, ginger powder and salt 

- Bring to boil on high flame and then for 15-20 min. on low flame

- Check occasionally, Reduce the water quantity to almost half

- Potato curry/Dum Aaloo is ready. Sprinkle some garam masala before serving

- Tastes best with plain steamed rice.

- **LOTUS STEM CURRY( ****Nadru**** ****Yakhni****)**

Nadru Yakhni is a traditional Kashmiri yogurt-based curry made with tender lotus stem

***I******ngredients****** ***

- Lotus Stem  500 grams, peeled and cut into 2 to 3 inches whole pieces

- Yoghurt (Curd): 2 cups/500 ml, fresh and whisked smooth 

- Mustard oil/Ghee: 3 tablespoons

Spices:

- 2 teaspoons fennel powder (saunf)

- 1 teaspoon dry ginger powder (soonth)

- 1 pinch asafoetida (hing)

- 2 black cardamoms(badi elaichi) (slit)

- 3–4 green cardamoms(choti elaichi) (slit)

- 4–6 cloves(laung)

- 1–2 cinnamon sticks (broken into small pieces)

- 1 bay leaf (tej patta)

- ½ teaspoon caraway seeds (shahi jeera)

- Salt: To taste

- Water: 1 to 2 cups 

**RECIPE****/**...

Prepare the Lotus Stem: 

- Peel the outer skin of the stem lightly. Wash thoroughly to clean dirt from the holes, and slice into 2 to 3 inch whole pieces.

- Boil the sliced lotus stems in water with a little salt ,fennel powder and ginger powder until tender (or pressure cook for 1–2 whistles)

- Add the whisked lump free yoghurt to the lotus stem on high flame and keep on stirring continuously to prevent curdling. Let  the curry thicken a bit

- Temper the Spices: Heat mustard oil or ghee in a heavy-bottomed pan. Add asafoetida, bay leaf, cinnamon, green and black cardamoms, cloves, and caraway seeds letting them crackle 

- Add the tempering to the lotus stem curry and let it cook for 5 minutes on slow flame so that the stem absorbs all the flavors. Finish with a light sprinkle of crushed dry mint leaves. And its ready, flavourful with golden creamy texture and light on the stomach.

- Undoubtedly tastes best with boiled rice.

**Crispy Fritters** made of **Lotus Stem (****Nadur**** ****Monje****)** often served with the most authentic flavourful cup of tea… **THE KEHWA**...a type of green tea

**LOTUS STEM FRITTERS** (Nadur Monje)

***Ingredients...***

- Lotus stem...250 gms

- Rice flour aprox.250gms, 

- 1 tsp Kashmiri red chilly powder

- ½ tsp ginger powder 

- ½ tsp,carrom seeds(ajwain)

- ½ tsp, salt/ to taste, 

- mustard oil

- little water

**Recipe****/ Instructions****..**

- Lightly peel the lotus stem and cut it aprox. into 2 inch pieces. Gently crush / beat it with a pestle to get rough thick finger chips like structure. Wash it thoroughly with water and let it drain. Sprinkle little salt on the cut lotus stem

- Prepare a thick batter using rice flour, red chilli powder, carom seeds, ginger powder, and salt.

- Dip the lotus stem pieces into the batter and deep-fry in hot mustard oil until golden and crunchy.

- Serve hot with a cup of  Kehwa (green tea)

**Authentic Kashmiri ****K****e****hwa** is a traditional green tea simmered with whole green cardamom, cinnamon, saffron, and crushed almonds. Unlike everyday milk teas, it is light, golden-amber, and poured fresh - often garnished with extra almond slivers and served to guests as a symbol of hospitality.

***Ingredients***

- Green Tea Base: Kashmiri green tea leaves or specialized loose-leaf green tea.

- Whole Spices: Crushed green cardamom, cinnamon sticks,

- Kashmiri saffron strands for aroma and a golden hue.

- Nuts: Sliced or crushed almonds as a garnish.

- Sweetener: Sugar or honey added to taste. 

How to **Prepare Classic ****Kehwa** (for 1 cup aprox 200 to 250 ml)

Boil Spices: Bring water to a boil with crushed cardamom (2) cinnamon (1/2 inch crushed), and saffron (4 strands). Let it simmer for a few minutes so that the flavors infuse. 

Add Green Tea (a little more than a pinch ,crushed generously between the palms of your hands)Turn off or lower the heat and cover with a lid gently for 2–3 minutes. Do not over-boil the green tea to avoid bitterness. 

Sweeten and Strain: Stir in sugar or honey (1 tsp.or as per the taste)then strain the golden liquid into cups

Garnish: Top each cup generously with crushed or slivered almonds before serving hot.

**No meal is complete without a dessert/sweet dish.**

Traditional Kashmiri dessert relies heavily on rich dry fruits, saffron, ghee, and slow-cooked milk or wheat. Iconic authentic recipes include 

**Kong ****Phirin**** **(saffron-infused semolina or rice pudding), 

Kong Phirin (Saffron Semolina Pudding)

**Kong ****Phirin**** **is a creamy, golden pudding prepared during Kashmiri festivals and family celebrations. 

***Ingredients: ***

- 1 cup semolina, 

- 4 cups milk,

- 1 cup sugar, 

- 2 tsp ghee, 

- a pinch of Kashmiri saffron strands, 

- 1 tsp cardamom powder, and 

- a mix of chopped almonds and pistachios. 

**Recipe/ Instructions....**

- Heat ghee in a heavy-bottomed pan and roast the semolina on low heat until it turns light golden and smells nutty.

- Warm the milk separately and slowly pour it into the roasted semolina, stirring constantly to prevent lumps.

- Cook on low heat until the mixture thickens into a smooth pudding.

- Stir in sugar, cardamom powder, and saffron strands.

- Pour into bowls, garnish generously with chopped nuts, and serve warm or chilled.
